# Complete Wilderness First Aid Guide

## 🚨 EMERGENCY ACTION PLAN

### PRIMARY ASSESSMENT (First 60 Seconds)

**DR ABC Protocol:**

**D - DANGER**: Is scene safe? (falling rocks, avalanche, wildlife)  
**R - RESPONSE**: "Can you hear me?" (tap shoulders)  
**A - AIRWAY**: Open, clear of obstructions?  
**B - BREATHING**: Look, listen, feel (10 seconds)  
**C - CIRCULATION**: Check pulse (carotid neck, 10 seconds)

**If unconscious + not breathing**: START CPR IMMEDIATELY

---

## 💔 CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

### Adult/Teen CPR (>12 years)

**Compression-Only CPR** (Preferred if untrained):
```
1. Place heel of hand on center of chest (between nipples)
2. Other hand on top, fingers interlaced
3. Arms straight, shoulders over hands
4. Push HARD and FAST
   - Depth: 5-6cm (2-2.4 inches)
   - Rate: 100-120/min (Bee Gees "Stayin' Alive" tempo)
5. Allow full chest recoil between compressions
6. Continue until:
   - Person breathes/moves
   - AED arrives
   - Professional help arrives
   - You're too exhausted
```

**CPR with Rescue Breaths** (If trained):
```
30 compressions : 2 breaths
- After 30 compressions, tilt head back
- Pinch nose, seal mouth
- 2 breaths (1 second each, chest should rise)
- Resume compressions immediately
- Minimize interruptions (<10 sec)
```

### Child CPR (1-12 years)
- Same technique, lighter pressure
- Depth: 5cm (2 inches)
- 1 or 2 hands depending on child size
- 30:2 ratio (compressions:breaths)

### Infant CPR (<1 year)
- 2 fingers on breastbone (just below nipple line)
- Depth: 4cm (1.5 inches)
- 30:2 ratio
- Gentle puffs for breaths (cheeks, not deep breaths)

**CRITICAL**: Continue CPR even if ribs crack (common, not fatal)

**Hypothermic victims**: "Not dead until warm and dead"  
- Continue CPR for hours if needed
- Cold slows metabolism (brain survives longer)

## 🩸 SEVERE BLEEDING

### Bleeding Control Hierarchy

**1. DIRECT PRESSURE** (First 10 minutes)
```
1. Expose wound (cut clothing away)
2. Apply gauze/clean cloth directly on wound
3. FIRM pressure with palm (don't peek!)
4. If bleeds through: Add MORE gauze (don't remove)
5. Hold pressure 10 full minutes (time it)
6. Elevate limb above heart if possible
```

**2. PRESSURE POINTS** (If direct pressure fails)
- **Arm**: Brachial artery (inner upper arm, against bone)
- **Leg**: Femoral artery (groin crease, against pelvis)
- Press firmly while maintaining direct pressure

**3. TOURNIQUET** (LAST RESORT - Life vs Limb)

**When to use:**
- Arterial bleeding (spurting blood)
- Direct pressure fails
- Amputation/mangled limb
- Multiple casualties (can't maintain pressure)

```
Application:
1. Place 5-7cm ABOVE wound (never on joint)
2. Use commercial tourniquet (CAT, SOFT-T) or improvise:
   - Belt, strap, triangular bandage folded (5cm wide minimum)
3. Tighten until bleeding STOPS (will be very tight)
4. Note TIME applied (write on tourniquet with marker)
5. DO NOT REMOVE (medical personnel only)
6. Check/retighten every 30 min if loosens

Improvised windlass: Stick through knot, twist to tighten

WARNING: 
- Limb may be lost (acceptable to save life)
- Pain will be severe
- NEVER apply for >2 hours without medical supervision
```

**Write on patient's forehead**: "TK" + time (e.g., "TK 1430")

## 🦴 FRACTURES & DISLOCATIONS

### Identifying Fractures

**Signs:**
- Deformity, unnatural position
- Severe pain, point tenderness
- Swelling, bruising
- Inability to bear weight/use limb
- Crepitus (grinding sensation)
- Shock symptoms

**Open Fracture** (Bone visible): 
- IMMEDIATE EVACUATION (infection risk)
- Cover with sterile dressing (don't push bone in)
- Splint as is

### Splinting Principles

**Rules:**
1. Splint where found (don't straighten unless no pulse)
2. Immobilize joint ABOVE and BELOW fracture
3. Pad splint (prevent pressure sores)
4. Check CSM (Circulation, Sensation, Movement):
   - **C**: Pulse distal to injury, warmth, color
   - **S**: "Can you feel this?" (touch toes/fingers)
   - **M**: "Wiggle toes/fingers"
5. Recheck CSM every 15 minutes
6. Loosen if swelling cuts circulation

**Improvised Splint Materials:**
- Trekking poles, sticks, tent poles
- Rolled newspaper, magazine
- SAM splint (moldable aluminum)
- Sleeping pad (foam)
- Duct tape + sticks

### Specific Fractures

**FEMUR (Thigh)**:
- Most dangerous (internal bleeding risk)
- Traction splint if available
- Or: Splint to opposite leg (pad between, tie together)
- Evacuate immediately (can bleed out internally)

**LOWER LEG**:
```
1. Remove boot carefully (swelling may prevent removal later)
2. Pad around leg
3. Place sticks/poles on sides
4. Tie above/below fracture + ankle/knee
5. Recheck pulse in foot
```

**ARM**:
```
1. Place padding in armpit
2. Bend arm 90° if possible
3. Splint along arm
4. Sling around neck
5. Bind arm to body with wrap around chest
```

**COLLARBONE**:
```
1. Arm sling (elbow 90°)
2. Swathe (strap binds arm to chest)
3. Pad between arm and chest
4. Can walk out
```

**ANKLE**:
```
1. Remove boot if severe swelling
2. Figure-8 wrap with elastic bandage
3. Improvised crutch (trekking poles)
4. Or: Splint boot on (tape/strap)
```

### Dislocations

**NEVER ATTEMPT IF:**
- Multiple injuries
- Fracture suspected
- No training

**SHOULDER** (Most common):
**If trained and recent injury (<30 min):**
```
Simple Traction Method:
1. Patient lies on back
2. Pull arm straight out from body (steady, firm)
3. May "pop" back (relief is immediate)
4. If not in after 3 min, STOP
5. Sling + evacuate

Success: 70% if immediate
Risk: Can worsen injury if fractured
```

**Untrained**: Sling + evacuate

## 🥶 COLD INJURIES

### Hypothermia

**Stages:**

| Temp | Symptoms | Treatment |
|------|----------|-----------|
| **35-32°C** Mild | Shivering, confusion, poor coordination | Remove wet clothes, warm drinks, insulate |
| **32-28°C** Moderate | Shivering stops, rigid muscles, slurred speech | Passive warming, skin-to-skin, heat packs |
| **<28°C** Severe | Unconscious, appears dead, rigid | CPR if no pulse, gentle handling, evacuate |

**Treatment Protocol:**

**MILD**:
1. Get out of wind/wet
2. Remove wet clothing
3. Replace with dry layers
4. Warm sugary drinks (NO alcohol/caffeine)
5. Insulate (sleeping bag, blankets)
6. Light exercise (if able)

**MODERATE**:
1. Handle gently (rough movement can cause cardiac arrest)
2. Remove wet clothes carefully
3. Wrap in dry insulation
4. Heat packs: Armpits, groin, neck (NOT directly on skin)
5. Skin-to-skin contact (strip, sleeping bag, 2 warm people)
6. NO hot drinks (can't swallow safely)
7. Monitor breathing constantly
8. Evacuate

**SEVERE**:
1. Treat as spinal injury (log-roll only, rigid spine)
2. Check pulse for 1 FULL MINUTE (may be very slow)
3. If no pulse: CPR (continue for hours if necessary)
4. Insulate (but DON'T add external heat)
5. Evacuate immediately
6. **"Not dead until warm and dead"**

**Rewarming Shock**: Rapid rewarming of severe hypothermia can cause cardiac arrest. Rewarm SLOWLY in hospital only.

### Frostbite

**Degrees:**
- **Frostnip**: White, numb, no blisters (reversible)
- **Superficial**: Blisters, purple/blue (may heal)
- **Deep**: Black, hard tissue (likely amputation)

**Field Treatment:**
```
1. Protect from refreezing (CRITICAL - worse than staying frozen)
2. Remove jewelry, tight clothing
3. DO NOT rub/massage
4. DO NOT rewarm if risk of refreezing
5. Evacuate frozen

If able to keep warm + prevent refreeze:
1. Immerse in warm water (37-39°C / 98-102°F)
2. NOT hot (causes tissue damage)
3. Continue 20-30 min until pink, pliable
4. Expect SEVERE pain (give ibuprofen)
5. Wrap gently with sterile gauze between digits
6. Elevate
7. Do NOT walk on thawed feet
```

**Why not rewarm in field**: 
- Refreezing causes MORE damage than staying frozen
- Frozen toes can walk out, thawed cannot

**Blisters**: DO NOT pop (infection risk)

## 🔥 HEAT INJURIES

### Heat Exhaustion

**Symptoms:**
- Heavy sweating, pale/cool skin
- Nausea, headache
- Weakness, dizziness
- Normal mental status

**Treatment:**
```
1. Move to shade
2. Remove excess clothing
3. Drink water (1L over 30 min)
4. Wet skin, fan
5. Elevate legs
6. Rest 30 minutes
7. Monitor (can progress to heat stroke)
```

### Heat Stroke (MEDICAL EMERGENCY!)

**Symptoms:**
- Hot, DRY skin (sweating stopped) OR still sweating
- Confusion, aggression, seizures
- Temperature >40°C (104°F)
- Rapid pulse

**Treatment:**
```
1. IMMEDIATE COOLING (minutes count)
2. Move to shade
3. Remove clothing
4. Pour water over body
5. Fan aggressively
6. Ice packs: Neck, armpits, groin
7. If conscious: Sips of water
8. EVACUATE URGENTLY (can cause brain damage/death)

Goal: Cool to 39°C (102°F) in 30 min
```

**DON'T WAIT**: Start cooling immediately, evacuate while cooling

### Dehydration

**Urine Color Chart:**
- Clear/light yellow = hydrated
- Dark yellow = mild dehydration
- Brown = severe dehydration

**Treatment:**
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS):
  ```
  Mix:
  - 1L clean water
  - 6 tsp sugar
  - 1/2 tsp salt
  
  Drink 250ml every 15 min
  ```

**Severe** (can't drink): Evacuate for IV fluids

## 🐍 BITES & STINGS

### Snake Bite

**Venomous vs Non-venomous** (if visible):
- Venomous: Triangular head, slit pupils, heat pits
- Non-venomous: Round head, round pupils
- **When in doubt**: Treat as venomous

**Treatment:**
```
1. Move away from snake calmly (avoid 2nd bite)
2. Remove jewelry, tight clothing (swelling)
3. Keep limb BELOW heart level
4. Clean wound gently
5. Immobilize limb (reduce venom spread)
6. Mark swelling edge with pen + time (tracks progression)
7. Walk slowly to medical care (don't run - increases spread)
8. Photo snake if safe (helps with antivenin)

Evacuate within 2 hours (critical window)
```

**DO NOT:**
- Cut wound ❌
- Suck venom ❌
- Apply tourniquet ❌ (unless elapid - coral snake, sea snake)
- Apply ice ❌
- Catch/kill snake ❌

**Australian Pressure Immobilization** (Elapids only):
- Wrap entire limb with elastic bandage (firm, not tight)
- Splint limb
- Slows lymphatic spread

### Tick Removal

**Proper Technique:**
```
1. Fine-tipped tweezers
2. Grasp tick close to skin (not body)
3. Pull straight up, steady pressure (don't twist)
4. Clean with antiseptic
5. Save tick in bag with date

DO NOT: Burn, petroleum jelly, nail polish (causes tick to regurgitate - increases disease risk)
```

**Lyme Disease**: Bulls-eye rash (days later) → medical care

### Bee/Wasp Stings

**Normal Reaction:**
```
1. Remove stinger (scrape with card edge, don't pinch)
2. Wash with soap/water
3. Ice 20 min
4. Antihistamine (diphenhydramine)
5. Pain relief (ibuprofen)
```

**ANAPHYLAXIS** (Life-threatening):
**Symptoms:** Throat tightness, difficulty breathing, swelling tongue, hives, dizziness

**Treatment:**
```
1. EpiPen to outer thigh (through clothing OK)
2. Press 3 seconds
3. Massage area 10 seconds
4. Lay person down, elevate legs
5. Second dose in 5-10 min if no improvement
6. CPR if stops breathing
7. EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY (even if improves)
```

**Carry 2 EpiPens if allergic** (1 may not be enough)

## 🤕 HEAD & SPINE INJURIES

### Concussion

**Signs:**
- Loss of consciousness (any duration)
- Confusion, memory loss
- Headache, vomiting
- Balance problems
- Sensitivity to light/noise

**Assessment** (every 30 min for 6 hours):
```
Questions (memory test):
- What happened?
- Where are we?
- What day/time is it?
- Repeat 3 words: "Ball, tree, car"

Tasks:
- Walk heel-to-toe (balance)
- Touch finger to nose (coordination)
- Pupils equal and reactive to light?
```

**Management:**
1. Rest (no screens, reading)
2. Wake every 2 hours overnight (check responsiveness)
3. Ibuprofen (NOT aspirin - bleeding risk)
4. Monitor for worsening

**EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY IF:**
- Unconscious >1 minute
- Pupils unequal
- Repeated vomiting
- Seizures
- Worsening headache
- Slurred speech
- Weakness one side

### Spinal Injury (Suspected)

**Mechanism of Injury** (assume spine injury if):
- Fall >1.5m (5 ft)
- High-speed collision
- Diving injury
- Unconscious from trauma
- Head/neck/back pain

**DO NOT MOVE** unless:
- Immediate danger (fire, avalanche, rising water)
- Airway blocked (need to roll for CPR)

**Immobilization:**
```
1. Tell person: "Don't move your head"
2. Manual stabilization: Hold head still from behind
3. Assess: "Can you feel this? Wiggle toes?"
4. Maintain head alignment with body
5. If must move: Log-roll with 3-4 people, move as unit
6. Improvised collar: SAM splint, rolled towel
```

**Airway over spine**: If not breathing, open airway (jaw thrust method, not head tilt)

## 🔥 BURNS

### Degrees

**First-degree**: Red, painful (sunburn)  
**Second-degree**: Blisters, very painful  
**Third-degree**: White/charred, painless (nerve damage)

### Treatment

**IMMEDIATE**:
```
1. Remove from heat source
2. Remove jewelry, clothing (unless stuck)
3. Cool with water 10-20 min (NOT ice)
4. DO NOT pop blisters
5. Cover with sterile dressing (non-stick)
6. Pain relief (ibuprofen)
```

**Rule of 9's** (Body Surface Area):
- Head: 9%
- Each arm: 9%
- Chest: 18%
- Back: 18%
- Each leg: 18%
- Genitals: 1%

**EVACUATE IF:**
- >10% BSA (larger than person's back)
- Third-degree (any size)
- Face, hands, feet, genitals, joints
- Chemical or electrical burn

**Do NOT apply**: Butter, oil, ointments (traps heat)

## 🦷 DENTAL EMERGENCIES

### Knocked-Out Tooth

**Time is critical** (30 min = 90% success, 2h = 10% success):
```
1. Find tooth (hold by crown, NOT root)
2. Rinse gently (don't scrub)
3. Reinsert if possible (patient bites on gauze)
4. If can't reinsert: Store in:
   - Milk (best)
   - Saliva (under tongue)
   - Saline
5. See dentist within 30 min
```

### Severe Toothache

**Temporary Relief:**
1. Floss (remove trapped food)
2. Salt water rinse
3. Ibuprofen 400mg + acetaminophen 1000mg (combo better)
4. Clove oil (apply with cotton ball)
5. Avoid: Hot, cold, sweet foods

**Abscess** (swelling, fever): Antibiotics needed, evacuate

## 💊 MEDICATION GUIDE

### Pain Relief

| Drug | Dose | Interval | Notes |
|------|------|----------|--------|
| Ibuprofen | 400mg | Every 6h | Anti-inflammatory, with food |
| Acetaminophen | 1000mg | Every 6h | Safer than ibuprofen, less effective |
| Aspirin | 325mg | Every 6h | Heart attack: Chew 324mg |

**Combo**: Ibuprofen + Acetaminophen = stronger than either alone

### Altitude

**Diamox** (Acetazolamide):
- Dose: 125mg twice daily
- Start: 24h before ascent
- Helps acclimatization, reduces AMS

**Dexamethasone** (Emergency only):
- HACE: 8mg, then 4mg every 6h
- Buys time to descend (does NOT cure)

### Allergies

**Diphenhydramine** (Benadryl):
- 25-50mg every 4-6h
- Causes drowsiness

**EpiPen**: 
- 0.3mg adults, 0.15mg children
- Outer thigh, through clothing
- Can repeat in 5-10 min

### Infection

**Antibiotics** (Prescription required):
- **Augmentin**: Broad-spectrum, wound infections
- **Azithromycin**: Respiratory, traveler's diarrhea
- **Cipro**: UTI, severe diarrhea

Take FULL course even if feel better

## 📋 SOAP Note (Document Incidents)

```
S - SUBJECTIVE (What patient says)
   "My ankle hurts, I fell 3 meters"

O - OBJECTIVE (What you observe)
   Swelling lateral ankle, tender to touch,
   can't bear weight, no deformity

A - ASSESSMENT (Your conclusion)
   Likely ankle fracture or severe sprain

P - PLAN (Treatment & evacuation)
   Splinted with SAM splint, ibuprofen 400mg,
   evacuating by litter carry
```

**Document**: Time, vitals, treatment, patient response

**Vitals Every 15 min:**
- LOC (responsive, verbal, pain, unresponsive)
- Pulse (rate, strength)
- Breathing (rate, quality)
- Skin (color, temp, moisture)
- Pupils (size, reactivity, equal?)

## 🚁 WHEN TO EVACUATE

**IMMEDIATE Evacuation:**
- Cardiac arrest, chest pain
- Severe bleeding (uncontrolled)
- Unconscious >1 min
- Difficulty breathing
- Spinal injury
- Open fracture
- Heat stroke
- Severe hypothermia
- Anaphylaxis
- Seizures
- Altered mental status

**URGENT Evacuation** (Within 6 hours):
- Closed fracture (unable to walk)
- Concussion (vomiting, confusion)
- Animal bite (rabies risk)
- Snake bite
- Severe pain
- High fever >39°C
- Moderate hypothermia
- Dehydration (can't drink)

**NON-URGENT** (Can walk out):
- Minor sprains
- Blisters
- Mild dehydration
- Minor cuts
- Simple fractures (finger, toe)

## 📞 CALLING FOR HELP

**Information to Provide:**
1. **Location**: GPS coordinates, landmarks, trail name/mile
2. **Number of patients**: "2 injured, 4 uninjured"
3. **Nature of injuries**: "Femur fracture, open"
4. **Treatment provided**: "Splinted, bleeding controlled"
5. **Patient vitals**: "Conscious, pulse 90, breathing 16"
6. **Weather conditions**: "Clear, wind 20km/h"
7. **LZ description** (helicopter): "Flat area 30m × 30m, no obstacles"
8. **Ability to reach patient**: "Off-trail, requires technical rescue"

**Emergency Numbers:**
- **112**: International (works without SIM)
- **911**: US/Canada
- Text 911 (if no signal, may work)
- Satellite SOS (SPOT/inReach)

**LZ Marking** (Helicopter):
- Clear 30m × 30m (100ft × 100ft) minimum
- No overhead wires, trees
- Slope <10°
- Mark center with bright clothing (weight down)
- Turn away, protect eyes from rotor wash

## 🧠 STAYING CALM (Provider Stress Management)

**As First Aid Provider:**

1. **Deep Breaths**: 4 counts in, 4 hold, 4 out
2. **Talk Through It**: "OK, first I'll control bleeding, then..."
3. **Focus on Tasks**: What can I do NOW?
4. **Delegate**: "You hold pressure. You call for help."
5. **Accept Limits**: You can only do what you're trained for

**After Incident:**
- **Debrief**: Talk through what happened
- **Normal Reactions**: Shaking, crying, anger = normal
- **Seek Support**: Don't bottle it up

**Remember**: 
- "I can only do my best with what I have"
- Improvised aid is better than no aid
- Most injuries are survivable with basic care

## 🏆 RECOMMENDED TRAINING

1. **Wilderness First Aid** (WFA): 2-day course
   - Basic injuries, CPR, evacuation
   - Minimum for backcountry travel

2. **Wilderness First Responder** (WFR): 8-10 day course
   - Extended care (24+ hours)
   - Advanced assessment, improvisation
   - For guides, expedition leaders

3. **Wilderness EMT** (WEMT): 5 weeks
   - Professional level
   - Medications, advanced procedures

**Recertify Every 2-3 Years**: Skills degrade without practice

**Practice:**
- Monthly scenario training with friends
- Review algorithms
- Carry reference cards

## 📚 QUICK REFERENCE ALGORITHMS

**Unconscious Patient:**
```
1. Scene safe?
2. Responsive? (tap shoulders, "Can you hear me?")
3. Breathing? (look/listen/feel 10 sec)
4. Pulse? (carotid 10 sec)
   - NO: Start CPR
   - YES: Recovery position, monitor
```

**Bleeding:**
```
1. Direct pressure 10 min
2. Still bleeding? Pressure point + direct pressure
3. Still bleeding? Tourniquet (life-threatening only)
```

**Fracture:**
```
1. Check CSM (Circulation, Sensation, Movement)
2. Splint where found (unless no pulse)
3. Immobilize joint above + below
4. Recheck CSM every 15 min
5. Evacuate
```

**Hypothermia:**
```
1. Get to shelter, remove wet clothes
2. Insulate, warm drinks (if mild)
3. Skin-to-skin, heat packs (if moderate)
4. Gentle handling, CPR if needed (if severe)
5. Evacuate (moderate/severe)
```

**Snake Bite:**
```
1. Move away from snake
2. Remove jewelry
3. Clean wound
4. Immobilize limb below heart
5. Mark swelling, note time
6. Evacuate within 2 hours
```

## ⚠️ LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS

**Good Samaritan Laws**: Protect responders from liability (most jurisdictions)

**Requirements:**
1. Act in good faith
2. No gross negligence
3. Within scope of training
4. Don't expect payment

**Consent:**
- **Expressed**: "Can I help you?" "Yes."
- **Implied**: Unconscious patient (assumed would want help)
- **Minor**: Treat first, find parent after

**Document Everything**: SOAP notes protect you legally
