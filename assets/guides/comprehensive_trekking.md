## Pre-Trek Preparation

### Physical Conditioning
**6-8 Weeks Before Trek:**
- **Cardiovascular Training**: 30-45 min cardio, 4-5 days/week
- **Strength Training**: Focus on legs, core, back
  - Squats: 3 sets × 15 reps
  - Lunges: 3 sets × 12 reps each leg
  - Planks: 3 sets × 60 seconds
  - Step-ups: 3 sets × 15 reps
- **Weekend Practice Hikes**: Gradually increase distance and elevation
- **Loaded Pack Training**: Add 5kg every 2 weeks (max 15kg)

### Medical Preparation
**Vaccination Schedule:**
- Hepatitis A & B
- Typhoid
- Tetanus booster
- Rabies (high-altitude regions)
- Yellow Fever (specific regions)

**Altitude Medications:**
- Diamox (Acetazolamide): 125mg twice daily, start 24h before ascent
- Dexamethasone: Emergency only, 4mg every 6h
- Ibuprofen: Pain/inflammation, 400mg as needed

**Pre-existing Conditions:**
- Heart disease: Obtain cardiologist clearance
- Asthma: Carry 2 inhalers + spacer
- Diabetes: Pack 2× insulin, test strips
- Allergies: EpiPen + antihistamines

## Essential Skills

### Navigation Basics

**Map Reading:**
1. **Contour Lines**: 
   - Close lines = steep terrain
   - 50m intervals (standard)
   - V-shapes point uphill in valleys
2. **Grid References**: 6-figure = 100m precision
3. **Magnetic Declination**: Adjust compass for true north
4. **Distance Estimation**: 1km = 12-15 min flat, 30 min uphill

**Compass Navigation:**
```
Step-by-Step Bearing:
1. Orient map to north
2. Place compass edge on route line
3. Rotate bezel until N aligns with map north
4. Add/subtract declination
5. Hold compass flat, turn body until needle aligns
6. Follow direction of travel arrow
```

**GPS Best Practices:**
- Download offline maps (2-3 sources)
- Waypoint every 500m
- Battery: Carry 2 power banks (20,000mAh each)
- Backup paper map always

### Water Management

**Daily Water Needs:**
- Moderate climate: 2-3L
- Hot/humid: 4-6L
- High altitude (>3000m): 4-5L
- Hard exertion: +1L per hour

**Purification Methods:**
1. **Boiling**: 1 min (sea level), +1 min per 1000m altitude
2. **Chemical**: 
   - Iodine tablets: 30 min wait
   - Chlorine dioxide: 4h for Crypto
3. **Filtration**: 0.1-0.2 micron filter (Sawyer, Katadyn)
4. **UV**: SteriPEN, 90 seconds per liter

**Water Sources Priority:**
1. Springs (cleanest)
2. Fast-moving streams
3. Lakes/ponds
4. Rivers (last resort)
**Avoid**: Stagnant water, downstream of camps, animal watering holes

## Safety & Navigation

### Weather Assessment

**Cloud Reading:**
- **Cumulus**: Fair weather, fluffy clouds
- **Cumulonimbus**: Thunderstorms imminent, evacuate ridges
- **Stratus**: Overcast, possible rain
- **Lenticular** (mountains): High winds aloft, storm coming 12-24h

**Wind Speed Estimation:**
| Beaufort | Speed (km/h) | Signs |
|----------|--------------|--------|
| 0-2 | 0-11 | Smoke rises vertically |
| 3-4 | 12-28 | Leaves/twigs move |
| 5-6 | 29-49 | Small trees sway |
| 7-8 | 50-74 | Walking difficult |
| 9+ | 75+ | Structural damage, EVACUATE |

**Temperature & Windchill:**
```
Windchill Formula:
35.74 + 0.6215T - 35.75(V^0.16) + 0.4275T(V^0.16)
Where T = temp (°C), V = wind speed (km/h)

Example: 0°C + 50km/h wind = -15°C windchill
```

### Altitude Sickness Prevention

**Acclimatization Schedule:**
- **Day 1-2**: 2500m, rest
- **Day 3-4**: 3000m (+500m max)
- **Day 5+**: +300m per day above 3000m
- **Rest Day**: Every 1000m gain

**AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness) Symptoms:**
- Headache (primary symptom)
- Nausea, vomiting
- Fatigue, weakness
- Dizziness
- Shortness of breath

**Lake Louise Score** (Self-Assessment):
| Symptom | Score 0 | Score 1 | Score 2 | Score 3 |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Headache | None | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
| GI upset | None | Poor appetite | Nausea | Vomiting |
| Fatigue | None | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
| Dizzy | None | Mild | Moderate | Incapacitating |

**Score >3 = Stop ascending, Score >6 = Descend immediately**

**HACE/HAPE** (Life-Threatening):
- **HACE**: Confusion, ataxia (can't walk heel-to-toe), altered consciousness
  - **Treatment**: Descend 500-1000m IMMEDIATELY + Dexamethasone 8mg + oxygen
- **HAPE**: Gurgling breath, cough with pink/frothy sputum, cyanosis
  - **Treatment**: Descend IMMEDIATELY + Nifedipine 30mg + oxygen

## Emergency Procedures

### Rescue Signals

**International Distress Signals:**
1. **SOS**: 3 short, 3 long, 3 short (light/sound)
2. **Mirror Flash**: 3 flashes, pause, repeat
3. **Whistle**: 3 blasts, 1 min pause, repeat
4. **Ground-to-Air**:
   - **X** = Need medical help
   - **V** = Need assistance
   - **→** = Proceeding this direction
   - **LL** = All well

**SPOT/InReach Devices:**
- **Check-in**: Daily 6pm
- **Help**: Non-life-threatening
- **SOS**: Emergency, activates GEOS rescue

### Shelter Building

**Emergency Tarp Shelter** (30 min):
```
Materials: 3m × 3m tarp, 10m paracord, 4 stakes

1. Find 2 trees 3m apart
2. Tie ridgeline between trees (1.5m height)
3. Drape tarp over line
4. Stake corners at 45° angle
5. Create door with extra cord

Wind Direction: Open side away from wind
Insulation: Pine needles, leaves (20cm layer)
```

**Snow Cave** (2-3 hours):
1. Find deep snow drift (2m+ depth)
2. Dig entrance tunnel (1m long, upward slope)
3. Hollow dome (2m diameter, 1.5m height)
4. Smooth ceiling (prevents drips)
5. Create ventilation hole (10cm diameter)
6. Mark entrance with pole/flag

**Temperature**: Snow cave maintains -5°C even when -30°C outside

### First Aid Quick Reference

**Hypothermia:**
- **Mild** (32-35°C): Shivering, confusion
  - Remove wet clothes, warm drinks, shelter
- **Moderate** (28-32°C): No shivering, rigid muscles
  - Insulate, heat packs (armpits/groin), warm IV if available
- **Severe** (<28°C): Unconscious, no pulse
  - CPR, evacuate, DO NOT rewarm in field

**Snake Bite:**
1. Stay calm, immobilize limb
2. Remove jewelry (swelling)
3. Clean wound, loose bandage
4. Pressure immobilization (Australian method)
5. Evacuate, NO tourniquets/ice/cutting

**Broken Bones:**
1. **Assess**: Deformity, pain, swelling
2. **Splint**: Rigid material + padding
3. **Rule**: Splint joint above & below fracture
4. **Circulation Check**: Pulse, warmth, movement every 15 min

**Blisters:**
- **Prevention**: Moleskin on hot spots, change socks 2× daily
- **Small (<5mm)**: Leave intact, donut bandage
- **Large**: Sterilize needle, drain, antiseptic, blister bandage

## Camping Ethics (Leave No Trace)

1. **Camp 60m from water**: Prevent contamination
2. **Dispersed Camping**: Move site every night if possible
3. **Human Waste**: Cat hole 15cm deep, 60m from water
4. **Pack Out Toilet Paper**: Or burn completely
5. **Food Storage**: 
   - Bear hang: 4m high, 1m from tree
   - Bear canister: 100m from camp
6. **Fire**: Use stove, fire only in emergency
7. **Wildlife**: 
   - Store ALL scented items
   - Never feed animals
   - 100m from bears, 25m from others

## Gear Weight Optimization

**Base Weight** (without food/water/fuel):
- Ultralight: <4.5kg
- Lightweight: 4.5-6.8kg
- Traditional: 6.8-11kg

**Big 3 Upgrades** (50% of weight):
1. **Pack**: 0.5-1kg (Osprey Exos, Granite Gear Crown)
2. **Shelter**: 0.5-1kg (Big Agnes Copper Spur, Tarptent)
3. **Sleep System**: 1-1.5kg (Quilt + pad)

**Food Weight**:
- 500-700g per person per day
- Dehydrated meals: 150g breakfast, 200g dinner
- Snacks: 300-400g (nuts, bars, chocolate)

## Expert Tips

1. **Trekking Poles**: Save 25% energy on descents
2. **Electrolytes**: Add to every liter above 3000m
3. **Gaiters**: Essential for snow/mud/desert
4. **Microspikes**: 90% of traction for 10% of crampon weight
5. **Rest Step**: Pause after each step at high altitude
6. **Pressure Breathing**: Forceful exhale helps at altitude
7. **Layering**: 
   - Base: Merino wool
   - Mid: Fleece/down
   - Shell: Gore-Tex
8. **Break Schedule**: 10 min rest every 50 min
9. **Sunrise Start**: Avoid afternoon thunderstorms
10. **Pack Weight**: <20% body weight
